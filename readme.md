## 5. QUESTIONS YOU SHOULD ASK THEM

**About the role**
- What's the client's industry, and what team would I be sitting in?
- What are the two or three must-have skills they're screening hardest for?
- Is it onsite, hybrid or remote, and where is the client based?
- What does the day-to-day actually look like, more reporting and dashboards, or more modelling and data engineering?

**About the contract**
- How long is the initial contract, and is there extension or temp-to-perm potential?
- Is the rate quoted as a day rate plus super, or inclusive?
- Is it a daily-rate contractor engagement or a fixed-term PAYG arrangement?

**About the process**
- What are the next steps from here, and what's the timeline?
- How many interview stages does the client run, and what should I prepare for?
- Is there anything in my background you'd want me to strengthen or clarify before you put me forward?

**Relationship (smart to ask)**
- Beyond this role, do you regularly recruit data and BI contracts in Melbourne? I'd like to stay on your radar.

## REMINDERS

- Treat the recruiter as an ally. If you're easy to represent (clear pitch, clear rate, clear availability, no work-rights friction), they'll champion you and bring you more roles.
- Never bluff a tool. "I haven't used it, but I learn fast, how central is it?" beats getting caught at the client interview.
- Confirm before they submit you anywhere, so you're not double-represented.
- Send your CV straight after, clean filename (Phuong_Dang_Data_Analyst_CV.pdf).
- This is a screening chat, not a technical grilling. Deep technical questions come at the client stage.
